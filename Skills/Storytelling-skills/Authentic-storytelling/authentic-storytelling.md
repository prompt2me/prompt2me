# Authentic Storytelling

## Purpose

Create brand stories that are grounded in reality, aligned with the brand's genuine values and identity, supported by evidence, and emotionally compelling without manipulation.

The goal is **not** to make a brand sound authentic. The goal is to discover what is actually true, meaningful, distinctive, and defensible about the brand, and turn those truths into compelling narrative — without inventing, exaggerating, or performing authenticity.

**Core principle:** *Do not manufacture authenticity. Discover it, clarify it, and articulate it.*

Authentic storytelling aligns four dimensions:
1. **Reality** — what actually happened
2. **Identity** — who the brand actually is
3. **Meaning** — why those facts matter
4. **Expression** — how those truths are communicated

Why this matters: readers can tell the difference between a story that performs sincerity and one that demonstrates it. Every fabricated detail, generic claim, or manufactured emotional beat is a liability the moment a skeptical reader — or a competitor, journalist, or disappointed customer — checks it against reality. The discipline in this skill exists to make stories that hold up under scrutiny, not just stories that sound good on first read.

## How to use this skill

Work through these phases. Don't skip straight to writing — the quality of an authentic story depends almost entirely on what happens *before* drafting.

1. **Discover** — gather the facts, experiences, decisions, and evidence. See `references/discovery-process.md`.
2. **Classify** — sort every piece of information by how well-supported it is (see Truth Classification below). Never silently upgrade an inference to a fact.
3. **Identify** — find the strongest real tension, human stakes, turning point, value-in-action, and distinctive truth (part of the discovery process).
4. **Select** — choose the story mode and narrative angle that fits the audience and objective. See `references/story-modes.md`.
5. **Structure & Write** — build the story using only elements the evidence supports, in specific, human, restrained language. See `references/language-and-voice.md` for line-level writing guidance and the story architecture.
6. **Test & Revise** — run the Authenticity Stress Test and Revision Protocol, and check the story against the Quality-Control Checklist. See `references/stress-test-and-revision.md`.
7. **Adapt & Deliver** — adjust emphasis for the audience and channel without changing the underlying truth (see `references/audience-and-channel.md`), then deliver the story with authenticity notes wherever attribution, uncertainty, or verification matters.

If the user hands you a finished draft rather than raw facts, skip straight to step 6 (stress-test the draft) after quickly confirming what's fact-checkable versus not.

If the user hands you raw material (founder notes, transcripts, customer data, company history) with no draft, start at step 1 and ask clarifying questions about anything you can't verify from what they've given you — don't fill gaps with plausible-sounding invention.

## Truth Classification

Before using any piece of information in a story, classify it. This is the single most important discipline in this skill — it's what prevents a compelling story from becoming a dishonest one.

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Confirmed** | Directly supported by reliable evidence |
| **Reported** | Provided by a person/source but not independently verified |
| **Observed** | Directly observable behavior or circumstance |
| **Inferred** | A reasonable interpretation derived from available evidence |
| **Aspirational** | Something the brand wants to become or represent, not yet reality |
| **Unknown** | Insufficient information exists |

**Never silently convert:** Unknown → Fact. Inference → Fact. Aspiration → Current Reality.

When you don't have enough information to classify something, say so and ask, rather than guessing. When you present an inference or aspiration in a story, mark it as such in your authenticity notes to the user, even if the prose itself reads smoothly.

## Never Fabricate

Regardless of how good it would make the story, never invent: events, dialogue, statistics, testimonials, customer reactions, founder motivations, failures, conflicts, emotions, or outcomes. Never exaggerate a real result ("helped several customers" must not become "transformed thousands of lives"). Never universalize one customer's or one founder's experience into a general claim without evidence to support it. Never manufacture struggle or manufacture minor imperfections just to make the brand seem more human — include real difficulty only when it's genuinely meaningful to the story.

## The Six Authenticity Dimensions (quick reference)

Use these as a checklist while gathering material and while reviewing a draft:

- **Factual** — does the story accurately represent events, people, decisions, and outcomes?
- **Motivational** — are motivations labeled by their actual evidentiary status (stated / documented / observed / inferred / unknown), not stated as settled fact?
- **Behavioral** — does the brand's actual behavior (pricing, hiring, product decisions, customer service) support the identity the story claims?
- **Emotional** — does emotional meaning emerge from real circumstances, rather than being asserted because "a story needs emotion"?
- **Identity** — does the story reflect the organization's real personality, vocabulary, and worldview, rather than an imposed brand voice?
- **Strategic** — of the things that are true, is this the truth that best explains why the organization matters to this audience? (Factually accurate ≠ automatically worth telling.)

## Final Standard

The strongest authentic brand story produces this reaction: **"That sounds like them."** Not: "That sounds like good marketing."

The objective isn't audience admiration for the storytelling — it's audience recognition of the truth of the organization behind the story. Authenticity is not a tone of voice. It is the alignment between what a brand says, what it has experienced, what it values, and what it actually does.
