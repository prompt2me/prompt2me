# Stress Test, Revision, and Quality Control

Run this on every finished draft — whether you wrote it or the user is asking you to audit an existing one.

## Authenticity Stress Test

- **Truth Test** — Could the claim be verified?
- **Evidence Test** — What supports it?
- **Specificity Test** — Could another organization say the same thing?
- **Behavior Test** — Does the organization actually behave this way?
- **Consistency Test** — Does the story contradict known facts?
- **Human Test** — Are real people affected?
- **Emotional Test** — Is the emotion earned?
- **Cost Test** — Did the stated value require sacrifice?
- **Time Test** — Has the identity persisted through behavior over time?
- **Skeptic Test** — What would a skeptical audience challenge? Then: what evidence would answer that challenge?

If a draft fails several of these, that's a signal to go back to the discovery process rather than trying to patch the prose.

## Revision Protocol

Work through these passes in order — each pass has a distinct job, so don't try to do all of them at once:

1. **Truth** — Remove unsupported claims.
2. **Specificity** — Replace abstractions with concrete details.
3. **Human Stakes** — Clarify who is affected and why.
4. **Tension** — Clarify what was difficult, uncertain, or at stake.
5. **Values** — Show values through decisions and behavior, not adjectives.
6. **Distinctiveness** — Remove statements competitors could easily reuse.
7. **Emotion** — Strengthen emotional meaning without manufacturing emotion.
8. **Compression** — Remove unnecessary material.
9. **Voice** — Make the story sound like the actual brand, not a generic brand voice.
10. **Skepticism** — Read it as a skeptical audience would. Ask "Would I believe this?" then "What evidence would make me believe it?"

## Quality-Control Checklist

Before delivering an authentic brand story, confirm:

- [ ] The story is grounded in real information.
- [ ] No fictional events were introduced.
- [ ] No unsupported motivations were presented as facts.
- [ ] No fake testimonials or quotations were created.
- [ ] Emotional claims are supported by evidence.
- [ ] Values are demonstrated through behavior.
- [ ] The story contains meaningful specificity.
- [ ] The narrative has a clear central idea.
- [ ] Generic corporate language has been minimized.
- [ ] The brand's actual identity is preserved.
- [ ] The story contains meaningful human stakes.
- [ ] The story is strategically relevant.
- [ ] Claims are proportional to evidence.
- [ ] Inferences are distinguishable from facts (in your notes to the user, if not in the prose itself).
- [ ] Unknown information has not been silently invented.
- [ ] The story can withstand skeptical scrutiny.
- [ ] The narrative communicates rather than performs authenticity.

## Common Failure Modes

| Problem | Correction |
|---|---|
| **Manufactured authenticity** — the story deliberately tries to sound "real" | Replace authenticity signals with actual evidence |
| **Corporate purpose language** — abstract language replaces narrative | Demonstrate purpose through decisions and behavior |
| **Founder mythology** — the founder appears impossibly visionary | Preserve genuine uncertainty, experimentation, mistakes, and discovery when the evidence supports them |
| **Fake vulnerability** — minor imperfections strategically revealed to manufacture intimacy | Include difficulties only when they're genuinely meaningful to the story |
| **Generic hero narrative** — the brand is portrayed as the hero that solved everything | Recognize customers, employees, partners, circumstances, and complexity |
| **Excessive chronology** — the story becomes a timeline | Keep only events that change meaning, direction, or stakes |
| **Unsupported emotion** — the writer states how people felt without evidence | Use observable circumstances and documented reactions instead |
| **Marketing disguised as storytelling** — every paragraph becomes a sales message | Let the story establish meaning before introducing strategic relevance |
