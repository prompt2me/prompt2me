# Audience Adaptation, Channel Adaptation, and Story Selection

The rule across all of this: change the angle and emphasis, never the underlying truth. The same real story can lead with different details depending on who's reading and where, but it can't contain different facts depending on the audience.

## Audience Adaptation

- **Customers** — emphasize relevance, problem, outcome, human benefit.
- **Employees** — emphasize identity, culture, principles, behavior.
- **Investors** — emphasize insight, decisions, differentiation, evidence, strategic implications.
- **Partners** — emphasize principles, reliability, shared interests, behavior.
- **General audience** — emphasize human meaning, discovery, tension, broader relevance.

## Channel Adaptation

- **Website** — prioritize clarity and identity.
- **About Page** — prioritize origin, beliefs, values, and distinctive identity.
- **Campaign** — prioritize one meaningful narrative tension.
- **Social Media** — extract one revealing moment or insight.
- **Video** — prioritize scenes, pacing, dialogue when documented, and visual details.
- **Podcast** — prioritize voice, reflection, discovery, and conversational depth.
- **Speech** — prioritize memorable narrative beats and spoken rhythm.
- **Case Study** — prioritize evidence, transformation, and customer experience.

## Story Selection Framework

When there are multiple candidate stories to choose from, score each one 1–5 on these dimensions rather than picking the one that simply sounds most dramatic:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Truth | Is the story strongly supported by evidence? |
| Relevance | Does it matter to the audience? |
| Distinctiveness | Could competitors tell the same story? |
| Emotional resonance | Does it contain meaningful human stakes? |
| Strategic value | Does it reinforce positioning? |
| Memorability | Is there a detail people can remember? |
| Evidence density | Does the story contain concrete proof? |
| Brand alignment | Does it reflect actual identity? |

Prioritize stories that score well across multiple dimensions rather than a single story that scores very high on one (usually "dramatic") and low on the rest.
