# Quality System Reference

## 11-Pass Editorial System

### Pass 1: Central Idea
- Is there one dominant claim?
- Does the title reflect it?
- Does the opening introduce it?
- Does the ending return to it?

### Pass 2: Question
- Is the question specific?
- Does it contain a genuine puzzle?
- Is the article answering the question rather than avoiding it?

### Pass 3: Logic
- Are the premises clear?
- Does each conclusion follow?
- Are causal claims justified?
- Are assumptions visible?
- Are examples connected to the argument?

### Pass 4: Relevance
- Does every paragraph serve the central idea?
- Can any section be removed?
- Has the essay wandered into a second essay?

### Pass 5: Examples
- Do examples test the argument?
- Are they accurate?
- Are their limits explained?
- Are hypothetical examples clearly labeled?

### Pass 6: Objections
- Is the strongest objection included?
- Is it treated fairly?
- Does the thesis need narrowing?

### Pass 7: Clarity
- Are terms defined?
- Are abstract claims concrete?
- Can a general reader follow the reasoning?
- Are transitions explicit?

### Pass 8: Tone
- Is the voice confident without sounding superior?
- Is contrarianism earned?
- Is the writing reflective without becoming vague?
- Is the personal voice limited?
- Is humor, if any, restrained and relevant?

### Pass 9: Evidence
- Are facts sourced?
- Are quotations accurate?
- Are studies represented correctly?
- Are examples real or clearly hypothetical?
- Are interpretations labeled?

### Pass 10: Ending
- Does the essay resolve or refine the initial question?
- Does the final paragraph add understanding?
- Is the practical implication proportionate?
- Does the ending avoid generic inspiration?

### Pass 11: Originality
- Is the prose original?
- Are examples original?
- Are metaphors original?
- Does the structure avoid reproducing a recognizable writer's signature pattern?

## Common Failure Modes and Fixes

### Too Many Ideas
**Problem:** The essay discusses several interesting ideas but develops none.
**Fix:** Select one central idea and remove everything that does not support, test, or qualify it.

### Contrarianism Without Evidence
**Problem:** The argument is surprising but unsupported.
**Fix:** Explain the mechanism, provide evidence, and state the conditions under which the claim applies.

### Straw-Man Conventional View
**Problem:** The common belief is represented in a weak or exaggerated form.
**Fix:** State the strongest reasonable version before challenging it.

### Anecdote as Proof
**Problem:** One story is treated as evidence of a universal pattern.
**Fix:** Use the story as an illustration and identify what additional evidence would be needed.

### Famous Examples as Decoration
**Problem:** Well-known people or organizations are mentioned without analytical purpose.
**Fix:** State precisely what each example demonstrates.

### Abstract Prose
**Problem:** The essay sounds intelligent but does not make a claim that can be examined.
**Fix:** Add observations, mechanisms, distinctions, examples, and consequences.

### Excessive Qualification
**Problem:** Every sentence is hedged until the argument disappears.
**Fix:** Use strong language for strong evidence and careful language for uncertain claims.

### Forced Practicality
**Problem:** A philosophical essay ends with generic advice.
**Fix:** End with a sharper question, a specific decision rule, or a clearer way of seeing.

### Too Much Personal Voice
**Problem:** The writer becomes the subject of every paragraph.
**Fix:** Use personal experience only when it illuminates the argument.

### Conclusion Repetition
**Problem:** The final paragraph merely restates the introduction.
**Fix:** Return to the opening with a revised understanding or narrower claim.

## Final Quality Checklist

Before delivery, confirm:

- [ ] The essay investigates one clear question
- [ ] The topic has been converted into a central idea
- [ ] The thesis fits in one sentence
- [ ] The opening establishes a specific puzzle or observation
- [ ] The common assumption is represented fairly
- [ ] The missing variable is clearly identified
- [ ] The reasoning develops in visible steps
- [ ] Examples test or clarify the argument
- [ ] Hypothetical examples are clearly labeled
- [ ] The strongest objection is addressed
- [ ] The final claim is narrower or more precise when necessary
- [ ] Facts, interpretations, inferences, and predictions are distinguishable
- [ ] Scientific, historical, technical, and cultural claims are appropriately sourced
- [ ] Quotations are verified
- [ ] No personal experience has been invented
- [ ] The writing is conversational but not casual
- [ ] The contrarianism is earned rather than manufactured
- [ ] The prose avoids jargon and empty abstractions
- [ ] The essay does not force a practical lesson
- [ ] The conclusion returns to the opening question
- [ ] The article remains useful beyond the immediate news cycle
- [ ] The content is original and does not imitate a specific writer's exact style
- [ ] No unsupported claim is presented as established fact

## Originality Boundary

Do not reproduce:
- Signature phrases of identifiable writers
- Published sentences or recognizable openings
- Distinctive metaphors or unique personal anecdotes
- Characteristic rhetorical sequences
- Biographical experiences not provided by the user
- Memorable formulations readers would associate with a specific author

Use only broad, non-exclusive qualities: essay-based reasoning, reflective inquiry, clear exposition, productive skepticism, independent judgment, concrete examples, simple language, careful qualification, one-idea focus.

---

## Intellectual Humility Phrases

Use where evidence is limited:
- "One explanation is…"
- "The evidence supports a narrower claim…"
- "This comparison is useful up to a point…"
- "The argument becomes weaker when…"
- "It is tempting to conclude…, but…"
- "A different interpretation would emphasize…"
- "The available evidence does not establish…"
- "This is a practical heuristic, not a law."
- "The result depends on what we mean by…"

Do not hedge every sentence. Excessive qualification makes a clear argument unreadable.

---

## Ethical Boundaries

Do not:
- Invent facts, quotations, personal experiences, statistics, or sources
- Misrepresent research or use unsupported psychological claims
- Present speculation as reporting
- Make medical, legal, financial, or safety-critical claims without qualification
- Frame systemic problems as individual failures
- Use contrarianism to deny established evidence
- Defame people or organizations or hide commercial interests
- Treat one story as proof of a universal rule

When discussing self-improvement, recognize constraints: disability, illness, financial pressure, care responsibilities, workplace hierarchy, discrimination, unequal access to time and resources.

## Reusable Prompt Templates

### Long-Form Essay
```text
Write a [word count]-word online essay about [topic].

Audience: [audience]
Central question: [question]
Common assumption: [assumption]
Provisional thesis: [thesis]

Develop one central idea rather than summarizing the entire topic.

Include:
- A specific opening observation or puzzle
- A fair account of the conventional view
- The overlooked variable
- A step-by-step argument
- Two or three concrete examples
- The strongest serious objection
- A refined or narrowed conclusion
- Practical or intellectual implications
- A return to the opening question
- Sources or verification notes where needed

Use clear, reflective, conversational prose.
Do not imitate any identifiable writer.
Do not invent facts, quotations, personal experiences, statistics, or sources.
```

### Contrarian Essay
```text
Develop a contrarian but evidence-aware essay about [topic].

Start by stating the conventional belief fairly.
Explain why it seems plausible.
Show what it misses.
Present a stronger alternative explanation.
Test that explanation with examples.
Address the strongest objection.
Explain where the argument does and does not apply.
End with one practical or intellectual consequence.

Avoid false binaries, exaggerated claims, straw-man arguments, and provocation for its own sake.
```

### Philosophical Essay
```text
Write a reflective online essay about [philosophical question].

Begin with an ordinary situation rather than an abstract declaration.
Develop one central idea.
Define important terms.
Use philosophical sources as tools for thinking, not decorative authorities.
Connect the question to ordinary life without reducing it to self-help.
Acknowledge competing interpretations.
End with a sharper question or practical way of seeing.
```

### Technology or Business Essay
```text
Write a long-form online essay about [technology, business, or organizational issue].

Central question: [question]

Examine: incentives, constraints, feedback loops, what is measured, what is ignored,
who benefits, who bears the cost, short-term and long-term effects, the strongest counterargument.

Use examples carefully and distinguish documented facts from interpretation.
Develop one central idea throughout.
```

### Editing Prompt
```text
Edit the following essay for intellectual clarity and argumentative strength.

Check:
- Is there one central idea?
- Is the question specific?
- Is the conventional view represented fairly?
- Is the missing variable clear?
- Does each section advance the argument?
- Are the examples relevant?
- Is the strongest objection addressed?
- Are facts distinguished from interpretations?
- Are claims appropriately qualified?
- Is the ending a genuine development rather than repetition?
- Is the prose clear, reflective, and conversational?
- Is the work original?

Remove digressions, unsupported certainty, decorative abstractions, weak examples,
repetitive conclusions, and unnecessary jargon.
```

## Source Priorities

1. Primary sources
2. Original research
3. Official records and direct data
4. First-hand accounts
5. Scholarly books and articles
6. Reputable journalism
7. Expert commentary
8. Established reference sources

**Evaluate sources by:** authority, date, method, scope, bias, incentives, corroboration, relevance, and what the source does not measure. Do not assume a source is reliable merely because it agrees with the thesis.

## Evergreen Durable Questions

- How do incentives shape behavior?
- How do people decide what to work on?
- What makes a problem worth solving?
- How does expertise develop?
- Why do organizations become inefficient?
- What is the relationship between freedom and constraints?
- How do tools alter attention?
- What makes an idea durable?
- Why do people imitate visible success?
- How should we reason under uncertainty?

When using a current event, separate: immediate example / underlying mechanism / durable principle / conditions under which the principle applies.
