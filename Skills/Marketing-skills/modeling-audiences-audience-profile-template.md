# Audience Profile Template

This is the full output structure for an Audience Profile. Use it whenever producing a complete, standalone Audience Profile deliverable. For quick internal handoffs to another skill, use `references/handoffs-and-quick-card.md` instead — it's much shorter.

Use this exact structure unless the user requests another format. Omit sections that clearly don't apply (e.g. no persona needed, no contradictions found) rather than leaving them as empty scaffolding in the final deliverable — the blank tables below are placeholders to fill in, not required output.

## Audience profile quality rules

- Do not create a persona before identifying a meaningful audience pattern.
- Do not confuse a market with a segment.
- Do not confuse a segment with a persona.
- Do not confuse a customer problem with a product feature.
- Do not confuse a job-to-be-done with a task list.
- Do not confuse motivation with demographic identity.
- Do not use invented customer quotations.
- Do not use unsupported channel preferences.
- Do not over-segment without a marketing reason.
- Do not assume the buyer, user, decision-maker, and influencer are the same person.
- Do not treat all customers as equally valuable or equally suitable.
- Do not hide contradictory evidence.
- Do not use sensitive personal data unnecessarily.
- Do not infer protected characteristics.
- Do not recommend targeting based on sensitive characteristics without authorization and legal review.

## Audience profile update workflow

When new audience evidence is provided:

1. Identify the relevant segment or profile.
2. Record the source and date.
3. Compare the new evidence with existing findings.
4. Determine whether it confirms, weakens, contradicts, or expands the current model.
5. Update only the relevant sections.
6. Preserve previous findings when they remain useful.
7. Mark outdated or superseded findings.
8. Update confidence levels.
9. Add a Change Log entry.
10. Identify affected messaging, content, campaign, or strategy documents.
11. Recommend validation if the change could affect major decisions.

## Updating confidence

Increase confidence when:

- Multiple independent sources support the finding.
- Direct customer evidence supports the finding.
- Behavioral data supports stated behavior.
- A message or offer test confirms the finding.
- The finding remains consistent over time.

Decrease confidence when:

- The finding comes from one unverified opinion.
- Evidence is old or context-specific.
- Stated preferences conflict with behavior.
- Sources contradict one another.
- The segment is too broad.
- The conclusion depends on an unsupported assumption.
